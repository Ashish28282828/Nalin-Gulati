Project: Improving Sales Conversion Through Customer Engagement

Where and when: Executive In-Store, promotions for the company, Birmingham, United Kingdom (January 2023 - February 2025)

Background and Challenge

I worked as an In-store Executive promoting the products/services of the company in Birmingham, and in this role I had to communicate directly with UK customers, tell them about the offered products/services, and generate a sale out of our interaction. Most of the people who came to see the promotion did not buy anything; they just wanted to know more about it, and the task was to get them interested and make a sale out of it.

Goals and Objectives

  • Turn leads into customers and assist with revenue generation.
  • Get to know each customer's needs and suggest relevant products.
  • Maintain a good relationship with all customers.
  • Elevate the customer experience.

My Role

The responsibility of the promotion lay with me as far as customer handling was concerned. I used to approach customers and get them involved, ask them what their needs were, explain about the products and pricing, counter any objections, and close the sale.

My Approach

  • Take the customer into account first, then your product. The reason I started off by asking them what they needed is that my suggestion would suit them well.
  • Become knowledgeable about your product. Understanding all the information of the product helps to make honest and convincing suggestions.
  • Confront any hesitation. Taking into account their hesitation regarding costs and commitment, I have managed to be open about it and provide explanations.
  • Keep improving. By paying attention to what works, I will know how to proceed further.

Outcomes

  • Sales were reported to be £55,000 in a single month.
  • Improved communication, persuasive and customer relations skills which I am now using in travel sales at Mann Travel.

What I Learned

This experience has made me realize that sales depend on trust and comprehension rather than mere persuasion. Another lesson learned is to remain resilient in the face of rejections and to adjust my selling methods depending on the client. This experience is relevant to what I have been learning about management in terms of motivation and communication.

Post 1: Leadership in Group Work

What I learned. Group work in ICL made me realize that leadership is not so much about instructing but rather guiding and motivating individuals to achieve a common objective. Group development phases by Tuckman (1965) have provided me with a theoretical perspective to analyze my observations: I saw how my group went through the stage of role conflict before it started functioning effectively. In this regard, I learned to set clear roles, emphasize deadlines, and help struggling colleagues.

How it was useful. This has proven helpful in my coursework where it has helped us maintain discipline as a group. In addition to this, this trait has helped me realize one of my weaknesses whereby I end up taking up more responsibilities myself rather than delegating them. Self-awareness as discussed by Whetten and Cameron (2016) has helped me understand that my behavior stems from a sense of personal accountability. However, effective managers should also trust other people and build on their skills.

How I will apply it. In my current role at Mann Travel, I am able to collaborate with my co-workers, and so I will take up volunteering activities related to coordination, such as guiding a new staff member through the booking system. In addition, I will develop skills of delegation by articulating reasons why a task should be performed, and also seek feedback after delegation. My future career objective includes working in a leadership position in customer service or sales in New Zealand.

Post 2: Communication Skills

What I learned. It was revealed to me that effective communication is key to avoiding confusion and making teamwork much easier. According to Whetten and Cameron (2016), supportive communication involves exchanging information while maintaining the quality of the relationship. As a result of this concept, I started giving feedback and expressing my disagreement in a different manner. In team exercises conducted at ICL, I had an opportunity to express my ideas concisely, ask clarifying questions, and confirm that the whole group understood things equally.

How it was useful. I have learned from my background experience in customer services that communication with customers can be done through the telephone, e-mail, and face-to-face. However, studying at ICL made me realize that communicating with my colleagues requires another type of communication. When communicating with customers, I concentrate on meeting their needs, but when it comes to working within a team, I should listen to various opinions and come to consensus. It turned out that the technique of active listening, for example, restating what one’s colleague has said, helped to avoid the same discussions and saved time.

How I will apply it. I will apply these skills when working with other people as well as my superiors in the workplace, for instance, when sending an email following a meeting to summarize our decisions to avoid confusion among everybody. Additionally, I will apply these skills in my future management role when it comes to setting expectations and providing feedback, among others. To continuously improve myself, I will seek feedback on my communication skills in writing and presentations.

Post 3: Cultural Awareness

What I learned. Being in New Zealand helped me develop skills of understanding and respecting people of various nationalities and different cultures. Dimensions of culture by Hofstede (2001), which include power distance and individualism-collectivism among others, helped me articulate what I had already noticed when working in groups at ICL; everyone has a different attitude towards time, authority, and expressing themselves. Silence can mean deep thoughtfulness, while assertiveness can mean only eagerness and energy.

How it was useful. Through my interactions with customers and colleagues in India, France, UK, and now New Zealand, I had gained some experience regarding diversity. ICL provided me with a framework to help me understand diversity, and reminded me that Hofstede's dimensions are indicative of the tendencies rather than individual behavior. Therefore, one should avoid stereotyping when working with individuals from different cultures. When we respected others’ opinions and treated everyone equally, we were able to conduct productive group discussions.

How I will apply it. Customers in the tourism industry are diverse, and cultural awareness plays a key role in determining the level of quality that is offered in services. I will be using it to ensure that I pay attention to my clients, do not make any assumptions, and am able to tailor the way I present choices to them. As a future manager, I would like to build an inclusive group of employees who feel valued and can freely share their ideas.

Post 4: Ethics and Professionalism

What I learned. From my experiences at ICL, it has become clear that professionalism entails being truthful, responsible, respectful, and trustworthy. Ethics are not just about rule compliance. The business ethics frameworks introduced me to two criteria for evaluating an action: Is it consistent with ethical principles like honesty and fairness? Does the action help or hurt particular people or groups? Both criteria are better used together than alone.

How it was useful. This means being honest about completing assignments myself and referencing sources properly while doing so and being accountable for my part in the group. It also applies when dealing with sales and customer service. A customer who has been pressurized or tricked into purchasing a product is likely to come back only once, but an honest piece of advice creates a relationship that lasts. This was the case for me after I got five-star reviews by UK customers on Trustpilot.

How I will apply it. It is important for me to give customers the right information regarding the prices, terms and options despite the fact that a less favorable option would bring about a faster sale. Another thing is to safeguard customers’ privacy, which is in accordance with the Privacy Act 2020 in New Zealand. I will not hesitate to admit any mistake and ensure the customers’ trust and respect in me. As a manager, it is my desire to lead by example and be honest.

Post 5: Digital Professionalism

What I learned. In addition to realizing the importance of the online profile as part of the professional profile, I also found out about the danger that the social network can bring to a reputation if it's not used properly. Potential employers often examine candidates' online profiles before inviting them for an interview, and once something gets on the Internet, it becomes very hard to get rid of.

How it was useful. Following this learning, I enhanced my LinkedIn profile by adding my picture, a summary, my employment history, and my skills. I have also reviewed my privacy settings and become more cautious when posting my updates on a public profile. It has been helpful in allowing me to showcase my customer service and sales experience and has also provided me a platform for networking within the New Zealand travel industry.

How I will apply it. My plan is to update my LinkedIn profile, provide relevant industry content and connect with other professionals, classmates, and ex-colleagues. In addition, I will make sure to have separate personal and professional profiles and be careful about what I am posting and commenting on. One of the areas I would like to develop are my digital and analytical competencies by using the customer data and online tools to enhance customer service due to the digital transformation taking place in the travel and customer service industries.

Post 6: Peer Evaluation – Reflecting on Feedback from Classmates

Context. While working in groups and presenting in ICL, I solicited feedback from fellow students regarding my performance.

Summary of feedback. My classmates have pointed out that I express my thoughts effectively, provide relevant examples based on my professional experience and demonstrate high level of dedication towards group assignments. In addition to that, they believe that I could improve the organization of my thoughts in presentations and give others enough time to speak.

What I learned from it. From the feedback, I got to know that experience is a strength, but the examples have to be used in such a way that they are connected to management concepts in an appropriate manner. In addition, it helped me to understand that since I am very talkative, there is always a chance that I might eat up all the time of the group.

Changes I have made or will make. I always prepare presentation notes that include an introduction, three points, and a conclusion, and practice before making the presentation. When working in groups, I ensure that I concentrate on listening and encouraging other students to participate as well as relating the work to the theories. I will request feedback after the next presentation to know if my communication skills have improved.

Reflection. Feedback is now a learning experience for me, not a critique. In the future of my professional life, I intend to request feedback from my peers and managers often. This, I feel, will contribute towards making me a better communicator, a better team player, and an effective manager.
